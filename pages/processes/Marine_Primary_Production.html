Marine Primary Production

ECOSYSTEM scale
Marine primary production is the fixation of CO2 into organic biomass by phytoplankton in the sunlit surface waters of the ocean. Contributing roughly half of the biosphere's total net primary production (~50 Pg C yr-1), oceanic phytoplankton photosynthesis drives the marine food web, fuels the biological carbon pump, and links living and inorganic carbon stocks. Production is primarily limited by light availability and nutrient supply (nitrogen, phosphate, iron), which are in turn regulated by ocean circulation, mixed-layer dynamics, upwelling, and temperature-driven stratification. Climate change is altering these physical controls, with consequences for marine productivity and food web structure.
Also known as: marine NPP, ocean primary production, oceanic primary productivity, phytoplankton primary production

Mechanisms

Phytoplankton Photosynthesis
Oceanic phytoplankton—including diatoms, cyanobacteria, dinoflagellates, and coccolithophores—fix more than 100 million tons of CO2 daily into organic material in the euphotic zone. This photosynthetic production fuels marine food webs through sinking particles and grazing, and drives the ocean biological carbon pump.

Nutrient Limitation of Ocean NPP
Phytoplankton growth in most ocean surface waters is limited by the availability of nitrogen, phosphate, or iron, which are supplied by upwelling, mixing, atmospheric deposition, and biological recycling. Nutrient supply is regulated by physical ocean dynamics, making marine NPP tightly coupled to ocean circulation and stratification.

Climate-Driven Decline in Stratified Ocean Productivity
Climate warming is intensifying upper-ocean thermal stratification in low-latitude oceans, reducing the flux of deep, nutrient-rich water to the euphotic zone. This has produced a measurable decline in marine NPP in stratified tropical and subtropical regions, with implications for marine food webs and the ocean carbon sink.

Drivers & Stressors

Nutrient supply from upwelling and mixing ABIOTIC
Deep-water upwelling and turbulent mixing supply the nitrate, phosphate, and iron required for phytoplankton growth; upwelling intensity is the primary control on NPP in coastal productive zones such as the California, Peru, and Benguela currents.

Ocean warming and stratification CLIMATE
Anthropogenic warming intensifies upper-ocean thermal stratification, deepening the thermocline and reducing nutrient entrainment into the euphotic zone, driving NPP decline in tropical and subtropical oceans.

Light availability ABIOTIC
Photosynthetically active radiation at the sea surface, attenuated by water depth and turbidity, sets the lower boundary of the euphotic zone and limits phytoplankton production; seasonal light cycles drive polar ocean bloom dynamics.

Interventions

Reduction of coastal nutrient pollution MANAGEMENT
Reducing nitrogen and phosphorus inputs from agricultural runoff and wastewater prevents coastal eutrophication and hypoxia, maintaining balanced phytoplankton community composition and preventing harmful algal blooms that degrade marine food webs.

Marine protected areas in productive zones PROTECTION
Establishing marine protected areas in high-productivity upwelling zones and coastal ecosystems reduces fishing pressure on upper trophic levels, maintaining food web structure that supports efficient transfer of phytoplankton production to higher trophic levels.
