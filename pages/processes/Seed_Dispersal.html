Seed Dispersal

ECOSYSTEM scale
Seed dispersal is the movement of seeds away from the parent plant to new locations, a critical process governing plant population dynamics, community assembly, gene flow, and species range shifts. Seeds are dispersed by animals (zoochory), wind (anemochory), water (hydrochory), and other vectors. Animal seed dispersal—carried out by frugivorous vertebrates that ingest fleshy fruits and deposit seeds in their excreta—is the dominant dispersal mode for the majority of woody plant species in tropical and subtropical forests, and is increasingly recognized as the primary mechanism of long-distance dispersal. The spatial pattern of seed deposition (the seed shadow) determines plant recruitment opportunities, influences plant genetic structure, and drives community dynamics. Anthropogenic defaunation, habitat loss, and fragmentation are eroding seed dispersal mutualisms, with cascading consequences for forest regeneration and species persistence under climate change.
Also known as: zoochory, frugivory-mediated dispersal, anemochory, endozoochory, seed rain

Mechanisms

Frugivore-Mediated Endozoochorous Seed Dispersal
Frugivorous vertebrates (birds, mammals, reptiles) ingest fleshy fruits and disperse seeds in their feces at locations distant from the parent plant. Seed dispersal effectiveness depends on the gut retention time of seeds (determining dispersal distance), the number of seeds deposited per defecation event, and the quality of microhabitats where seeds are deposited relative to safe sites for germination. Effective seed dispersers are distinguished from pulp peckers (which consume pulp without ingesting seeds) and seed predators (which destroy seeds by mastication). Large-bodied frugivores disperse the largest-seeded fruits and achieve the greatest dispersal distances, making them disproportionately important for forest regeneration and gene flow.

Long-Distance Seed Dispersal Events
Long-distance dispersal (LDD) events — where seeds are transported many kilometers from the parent plant — are rare relative to typical seed shadows but disproportionately important for plant population connectivity, range expansion, and colonization of new habitats. LDD is driven by large and migratory animals, extreme meteorological events (storms, tornadoes), ocean currents, and human transport. LDD allows plant populations to track shifting climate envelopes and colonize habitat patches beyond typical dispersal distances, playing a key role in range expansions under climate change.

Seed Shadow Shape Determines Plant Recruitment and Spatial Structure
The spatial distribution of seeds around parent plants (the seed shadow) varies among plant species, populations, and individuals as a function of disperser identity, disperser home range size, seed retention time, and habitat structure. Short-distance clumped seed shadows create intense competition among recruits near parent plants; long-tailed seed shadows with rare long-distance events connect plant populations across landscapes. The shape of the seed shadow is the primary link between dispersal mechanism and plant community dynamics, gene flow, and succession.

Ecological Indicators

Seed trap density and distance from parent plant
Standardized seed traps (plastic trays, funnels) deployed under parent plant canopies and at increasing distances measure the spatial density distribution of seeds (the empirical seed shadow). Trap catch data fitted to dispersal kernel models estimate the mean and maximum dispersal distances and quantify the shape of the seed shadow (exponential decay vs. fat-tailed). This is the primary field method for quantifying seed dispersal distance and plant recruitment potential.
PATO concentration PATO:0000070 COMMON SEASONAL
Measurement: Seeds per seed trap per day; seed trap catches fitted to exponential or 2Dt dispersal kernels (seeds m-2 day-1 vs. distance m)

Frugivore diversity and abundance
Species richness, abundance, and functional diversity of frugivorous vertebrates (birds, mammals) in a plant community predict the diversity of seed shadows and the frequency of long-distance dispersal events. Monitoring frugivore communities provides a leading indicator of seed dispersal service provision, particularly in defaunated landscapes where disperser loss precedes measurable changes in plant recruitment.

Drivers & Stressors

Frugivore community composition and body size BIOTIC
The identity, body size, gut morphology, and behavioral ecology of frugivorous vertebrates determines the diversity of seed shadows produced in a plant community. Large-bodied frugivores achieve greater dispersal distances and disperse the largest-seeded plants. Frugivore community composition is shaped by habitat quality, hunting pressure, and ecosystem history, making it both a biological driver and an indicator of seed dispersal function.

Wind speed and turbulence for anemochorous species CLIMATE
For wind-dispersed plant species (with plumed, winged, or pappose seeds), wind speed, turbulence, and atmospheric boundary layer structure determine dispersal distance. Open-canopy habitats and elevated terrain enhance wind-mediated LDD; extreme weather events (storms, tornadoes) generate the rare, very long-distance events that drive metapopulation connectivity.

Defaunation and hunting of large frugivores ANTHROPOGENIC
Hunting and habitat loss disproportionately eliminate the largest-bodied frugivores (tapirs, large primates, hornbills, toucans), which are the primary or sole dispersers of the largest-seeded plant species. Defaunation of tropical forests creates dispersal deficits — reduced seed dispersal distances and missing seed shadows for large-seeded trees — threatening long-term forest regeneration and carbon stocks.

Interventions

Large frugivore conservation and hunting moratoriums PROTECTION
Protecting large frugivorous mammals (primates, tapirs, large ungulates) and birds (hornbills, toucans, large pigeons) through hunting moratoriums, protected area management, and anti-poaching enforcement preserves the keystone dispersers of large-seeded tree species. Frugivore protection maintains functional seed shadows and long-distance dispersal capacity essential for forest regeneration and tree species persistence under climate-driven range shifts.

Habitat corridor establishment to reconnect fragmented frugivore populations RESTORATION
Establishing riparian corridors, forest patches, and biodiversity-friendly matrix habitats between forest fragments enables frugivorous birds and mammals to move between patches, restoring seed dispersal connectivity. Functional landscape corridors allow frugivores to transport seeds across agricultural matrices, regenerating plant communities in isolated patches and facilitating climate-driven range shifts of plant species.
