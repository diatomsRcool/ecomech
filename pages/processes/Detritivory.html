Detritivory

ECOSYSTEM scale
Detritivory is the consumption and physical fragmentation of dead organic matter (detritus) by animals, accelerating decomposition by comminuting plant litter, stimulating microbial activity, and cycling nutrients back to the soil. Macro-detritivores—earthworms, isopods, millipedes, diplopods, and mites—mechanically fragment litter, increasing surface area for microbial attack, and deposit nutrient-enriched castings or feces that form hotspots of elevated nitrogen and phosphorus availability. In arid ecosystems, macro- detritivore activity can drive litter removal rates that far exceed what microbial decomposition alone can explain. Detritivory couples the aboveground and belowground subsystems of terrestrial ecosystems, linking plant litter quality to soil food web structure and nutrient mineralization rates.
Also known as: detrivory, detritus feeding, macrofaunal decomposition, litter fragmentation

Mechanisms

Physical Litter Fragmentation by Soil Macrofauna
Macro-detritivores (earthworms, isopods, millipedes, Diplopoda) ingest and mechanically fragment plant litter into fine particles, dramatically increasing the surface area available for microbial colonization and enzymatic attack. Earthworms additionally mix organic matter into the mineral soil matrix (bioturbation), creating organo-mineral associations that stabilize carbon and redistribute nutrients. Faunal fragmentation accelerates overall decomposition beyond what microbial activity achieves alone, particularly during periods of low microbial activity.

Detritivore Nutrient Mineralization via Excretion
Detritivores release inorganic nitrogen and phosphorus as excretory byproducts of litter digestion, creating localized nutrient enrichment zones around burrows, feeding sites, and casts. Earthworm casts and isopod feces contain higher concentrations of plant-available nutrients than surrounding soil, functioning as nutrient hotspots that stimulate plant uptake and microbial activity. In arid systems, detritivore excretion maintains nutrient cycling during dry periods when microbial activity is minimal.

Detritivore Coupling of Aboveground and Belowground Subsystems
Detritivores link aboveground plant litter production to belowground soil food web structure, nutrient cycling, and plant productivity through their selective feeding on litter of specific plant species. Litter quality (C:N ratio, palatability to detritivores) determines the rate of nutrient release, creating plant-soil feedbacks that reinforce or alter plant community composition over time. Detritivore community composition is shaped by plant species identity through litter quality, creating aboveground-belowground feedbacks mediated by the detritivore trophic level.

Ecological Indicators

Litter bag mass loss by macrofauna
Comparing mass loss in coarse-mesh (macrofauna access) vs. fine-mesh (microbial only) litterbags isolates the detritivore contribution to litter breakdown. The difference in mass loss between mesh sizes quantifies macro-detritivore fragmentation effects on decomposition rate.

Drivers & Stressors

Litter substrate quality ABIOTIC
Plant litter C:N ratio, N content, and functional diversity determine detritivore palatability, selectivity, and community response. High-N, low-C:N litter attracts and sustains higher detritivore biomass and accelerates nutrient cycling; recalcitrant, high-lignin litter is less palatable and decomposes more slowly.

Soil moisture and temperature CLIMATE
Soil moisture and temperature govern detritivore activity periods, with earthworms requiring moist soils and isopods active during nocturnal, cooler periods. In arid ecosystems, detritivore litter processing peaks during seasons when microbial decomposition is suppressed by drought, creating complementary detritivore-microbial activity windows.

Land-use intensification ANTHROPOGENIC
Agricultural tillage, pesticide use, and soil compaction reduce detritivore abundance and diversity. Intensive land use eliminates earthworm communities and disrupts macro-arthropod detritivore populations, reducing litter processing rates and nutrient cycling efficiency in managed landscapes.

Interventions

Reduced tillage and organic farming MANAGEMENT
Reduced tillage and organic farming practices sustain earthworm and macro-arthropod detritivore communities by preserving soil structure, reducing compaction and pesticide impacts. Higher detritivore abundance under low-tillage systems improves litter processing rates, nutrient cycling efficiency, and soil carbon accumulation.

Native vegetation restoration to improve litter quality RESTORATION
Restoring plant communities with diverse litter quality (C:N ratio, palatability) supports diverse detritivore communities and maximizes nutrient cycling through detritivore-mediated pathways. Diverse litter inputs matching detritivore feeding preferences accelerate litter processing and nutrient mineralization compared to monoculture litter.
