Terrestrial Primary Production

ECOSYSTEM scale
Terrestrial primary production is the fixation of atmospheric CO2 into organic biomass by land plants through photosynthesis. Gross primary production (GPP) represents the total carbon fixed, while net primary production (NPP) is GPP minus autotrophic respiration. At ~123 Pg C yr-1 globally, terrestrial GPP is the largest biospheric CO2 flux and the foundation of all terrestrial food webs and carbon cycling. Production is controlled by solar radiation, water availability, temperature, and nutrient supply, with tropical forests and savannahs accounting for ~60% of global GPP.
Also known as: terrestrial NPP, terrestrial net primary productivity, terrestrial GPP, land primary production

Mechanisms

Photosynthetic Carbon Fixation by Land Plants
Land plants capture solar energy via chlorophyll and fix atmospheric CO2 through the Calvin cycle, producing carbohydrates that fuel plant growth and ecosystem energy flow. This autotrophic process is the primary driver of terrestrial carbon uptake and is quantified globally at ~123 Pg C yr-1.

Water Limitation of Terrestrial NPP
Precipitation and soil moisture availability impose primary constraints on terrestrial NPP across dryland and seasonally dry ecosystems. Water stress causes stomatal closure, reducing CO2 uptake and photosynthetic rates. Large-scale droughts can reduce global NPP measurably, counteracting the stimulatory effects of warming on plant photosynthesis.

Tropical Dominance of Global Terrestrial GPP
Tropical forests and savannahs account for approximately 60% of global terrestrial GPP due to high solar irradiance, warm temperatures, and abundant precipitation in equatorial and subtropical regions. These biomes maintain high year-round photosynthetic rates, making them disproportionately important to the global carbon cycle.

Drivers & Stressors

Solar radiation (PAR) ABIOTIC
Photosynthetically active radiation (400-700 nm) is the energy source driving terrestrial photosynthesis; GPP increases linearly with absorbed PAR until other factors become limiting, making solar radiation the ultimate control on terrestrial primary production potential.

Precipitation and drought CLIMATE
Precipitation is the primary control on terrestrial NPP in dryland and seasonally dry biomes; drought is the dominant mechanism driving interannual to decadal NPP declines globally, capable of overriding warming-driven NPP gains.

Temperature CLIMATE
Temperature modulates enzymatic rates of photosynthesis and respiration; historically warming has increased NPP in the Northern Hemisphere, but this gain is countered by water limitation under drying trends.

Interventions

Forest restoration and reforestation RESTORATION
Restoring degraded forests and reforesting cleared land re-establishes high-productivity plant canopies, increasing terrestrial NPP and strengthening the terrestrial carbon sink as a climate mitigation strategy.

Drought-resilient land management MANAGEMENT
Adapting agricultural and forest management to increase water-use efficiency and resilience to drought (e.g., agroforestry, soil moisture conservation, drought-tolerant varieties) sustains terrestrial NPP under increasing climate aridity.
