Nitrogen Cycling

ECOSYSTEM scale
Nitrogen cycling encompasses the biogeochemical transformations of reactive and inert nitrogen through terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems. Key transformations include biological nitrogen fixation, nitrification, denitrification, ammonification, assimilation, and anaerobic ammonium oxidation (anammox). These processes regulate ecosystem productivity, water quality, and greenhouse gas (N2O) emissions, and are tightly coupled to carbon and phosphorus cycles.
Also known as: nitrogen biogeochemical cycle, N cycle, nitrogen biogeochemistry

Mechanisms

Biological Nitrogen Fixation
Conversion of atmospheric dinitrogen (N2) to bioavailable ammonium (NH4+) by diazotrophic microorganisms, either free-living in soil or in symbiosis with plant hosts. Catalyzed by the nitrogenase enzyme complex.

Nitrification
Two-step aerobic oxidation of ammonium to nitrite (by ammonia-oxidizing bacteria and archaea) and then to nitrate (by nitrite-oxidizing bacteria). Complete ammonia oxidizers (comammox) can perform both steps.

Denitrification
Anaerobic microbial reduction of nitrate and nitrite to gaseous nitrogen oxides (NO, N2O) and dinitrogen (N2), constituting the primary pathway for permanent N loss from ecosystems and a major source of the greenhouse gas nitrous oxide (N2O).

Ammonification (Mineralization)
Decomposition of organic nitrogen-containing compounds (proteins, nucleic acids) by heterotrophic microorganisms and fungi, releasing inorganic ammonium (NH4+). Constitutes the primary internal recycling pathway for organically bound N.

Drivers & Stressors

Atmospheric nitrogen deposition ANTHROPOGENIC
Anthropogenic emission of NOx and NH3 from combustion and agriculture increases reactive N inputs to ecosystems, accelerating nitrification, denitrification, and N leaching.

Soil moisture ABIOTIC
Water-filled pore space controls O2 diffusion, creating anaerobic microsites that promote denitrification; also influences microbial activity rates for nitrification and mineralization.

Temperature CLIMATE
Soil temperature is a primary control on the rates of all microbially mediated N transformations, with Q10 values of 1.5-2.5 for most nitrification and denitrification reactions.

Agricultural fertilization ANTHROPOGENIC
Application of synthetic N fertilizers (ammonium nitrate, urea) dramatically increases substrate availability for nitrification and denitrification, driving N2O emissions and nitrate leaching.

Interventions

Riparian buffer establishment RESTORATION
Planting vegetated buffers along waterways to intercept NO3- in subsurface flow, promoting denitrification and plant N uptake before N reaches streams.

Cover cropping MANAGEMENT
Growing leguminous or non-leguminous cover crops between cash crop seasons to immobilize mineral N in biomass, reducing nitrification and leaching losses over winter.

Constructed wetland treatment RESTORATION
Engineered wetland systems designed to maximize denitrification for nitrate removal from agricultural runoff or wastewater before discharge.
