Carbon Cycling

GLOBAL scale
Carbon cycling encompasses the biotic and abiotic transformations of carbon among the atmosphere, living biomass, soil organic matter, and aquatic systems. Photosynthesis (gross primary production) is the dominant carbon uptake flux; autotrophic and heterotrophic respiration, decomposition, and combustion return CO2 to the atmosphere. Terrestrial forests currently sequester ~2.4 Pg C yr⁻¹, partially offsetting anthropogenic fossil fuel emissions. Soil respiration is the second-largest terrestrial carbon flux and is sensitive to temperature, creating a potential positive feedback under climate change. Tropical land-use change (deforestation) is a major net carbon source. The global carbon cycle is tightly coupled to the nitrogen and phosphorus cycles.
Also known as: carbon biogeochemical cycle, C cycle, terrestrial carbon cycle

Mechanisms

Gross Primary Production (Photosynthesis)
Autotrophic organisms—principally vascular plants, phytoplankton, and cyanobacteria—fix atmospheric CO2 into organic carbon via photosynthesis. Terrestrial GPP is estimated at ~123 Pg C yr⁻¹ globally, making it the largest global CO2 flux. GPP is strongly controlled by light, temperature, precipitation, and nutrient availability; tropical forests and savannahs account for ~60% of terrestrial GPP.

Ecosystem Respiration and Soil Carbon Decomposition
Autotrophic (plant) and heterotrophic (microbial and faunal) respiration return organic carbon to the atmosphere as CO2. Soil respiration—the sum of root respiration and microbial decomposition of soil organic matter—is the second-largest terrestrial carbon flux at ~98 Pg C yr⁻¹ globally, and increases with temperature (Q10 ≈ 1.5), potentially constituting a positive feedback to climate warming.

Forest Biomass Carbon Sequestration
Net accumulation of carbon in living forest biomass (aboveground and belowground) represents the primary long-term terrestrial carbon sink. Global intact forests sequester ~2.4 Pg C yr⁻¹, partially offsetting fossil fuel emissions, while tropical deforestation releases ~1.3 Pg C yr⁻¹. Carbon residence time in biomass ranges from decades (fast-growing tropical trees) to centuries (old-growth temperate and boreal forests).

Ecological Indicators

Net Ecosystem Production
NEP is the net carbon balance between GPP and total ecosystem respiration (autotrophic + heterotrophic), measured by eddy covariance flux towers. A positive NEP indicates net carbon uptake; negative NEP indicates a net carbon source. NEP integrates both photosynthetic and respiratory components of ecosystem carbon cycling.

Soil organic carbon stock
The mass of organic carbon stored in the soil profile (0–100 cm) represents the largest terrestrial carbon pool (~1,500 Pg C in the top meter). Changes in soil organic carbon stocks integrate the balance between organic matter inputs and decomposition and are a key indicator of ecosystem carbon sequestration or loss.

Atmospheric CO2 concentration trend
Atmospheric CO2 concentration (ppm) measured at background monitoring stations (e.g., Mauna Loa) provides a global integrator of all carbon cycle fluxes. Seasonal drawdown reflects GPP; the long-term annual increase reflects the imbalance between anthropogenic emissions and natural sinks.

Drivers & Stressors

Temperature CLIMATE
Temperature is the primary climatic control on both GPP (via enzymatic rates and growing season length) and respiration (via microbial and autotrophic metabolic rates), with the net effect on the carbon balance depending on the relative sensitivity of production vs. decomposition.

Precipitation and soil moisture CLIMATE
Water availability limits GPP over 40% of the vegetated land surface, particularly in semi-arid and seasonally dry ecosystems. Drought reduces stomatal conductance and photosynthesis while also modulating microbial respiration rates.

Land-use change and deforestation ANTHROPOGENIC
Conversion of forest to agriculture is the second-largest anthropogenic CO2 emission source after fossil fuels. Tropical deforestation releases stored biomass and soil carbon while simultaneously eliminating the ongoing forest carbon sink.

Atmospheric CO2 fertilization ANTHROPOGENIC
Rising atmospheric CO2 concentrations increase photosynthetic carbon fixation rates in many plant species (the CO2 fertilization effect), contributing to the observed enhancement of the global terrestrial carbon sink over recent decades.

Interventions

Reforestation and forest restoration RESTORATION
Restoration of degraded forests and reforestation of former agricultural land restores the biomass carbon sink, with tropical reforestation offering the highest rates of carbon sequestration per unit area.

Reduced tropical deforestation PROTECTION
Policies and programs that reduce deforestation rates in tropical regions (REDD+) avoid large carbon emissions from biomass combustion and decomposition while maintaining ongoing forest carbon sinks and ecosystem services.

Soil carbon management through cover cropping MANAGEMENT
Growing cover crops during fallow periods increases organic matter inputs to soil, builds soil organic carbon stocks, and reduces decomposition of existing SOC by maintaining soil biological activity and structure.
