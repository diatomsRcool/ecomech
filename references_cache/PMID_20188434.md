Pollinators are a key component of global biodiversity, providing vital 
ecosystem services to crops and wild plants. There is clear evidence of recent 
declines in both wild and domesticated pollinators, and parallel declines in the 
plants that rely upon them. Here we describe the nature and extent of reported 
declines, and review the potential drivers of pollinator loss, including habitat 
loss and fragmentation, agrochemicals, pathogens, alien species, climate change 
and the interactions between them. Pollinator declines can result in loss of 
pollination services which have important negative ecological and economic 
impacts that could significantly affect the maintenance of wild plant diversity, 
wider ecosystem stability, crop production, food security and human welfare.
